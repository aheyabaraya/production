# Camera Coverage Vocabulary

Load this module at the registered camera/composition reference checkpoint, the
Graph P3 section-kinetic and internal FP Close model checkpoints, 01
core-direction checkpoint, 02A coverage checkpoint, and 03V motion-direction
checkpoint. Do not load it at Founder Direction Alignment. The same vocabulary
crosses those decisions, but authority does
not: P3 / FP Close authors four-axis geometry onto Core-bound change-point
slots; reference research observes source evidence, 01/02A reserve a generatable
still view and motion-supporting geometry, and 03V alone authors actual
temporal camera movement after passed pixels exist. 02B does not reopen this
full catalog; it exact-translates the approved 02A still geometry and visible
readiness consequences through its compact five-line prompt instructions.

This is a camera-decision vocabulary, not a shot template. Select only the
families the current song, screen event, member/object relation, and transition
need. Custom current-run labels are allowed when the visible geometry and job
remain explicit.

## Quick Selection Guide

Use this top layer to make the decision. Descend to the detailed vocabulary
only when a physical relation or a confusing technique pair needs definition.
Do not scan the catalog to harvest stylish terms, and stop browsing once the
current screen job, geometry, continuity, and stage authority are explicit.

### Thirty-Second Selection Pass

Choose camera coverage in this order:

```text
audio/section cue and screen job
-> camera relation to subject/object/world
-> height + distance/scale + direction/angle + depth
-> composition + lens/focus feel
-> motion readiness: axis, travel/reveal room, layers, path, and crop safety
-> entry/exit continuity and edit handoff
-> after pixel PASS only: 03V movement or hold + speed + envelope + stop
```

Technique words never replace geometry. `dynamic`, `cinematic`, `tracking`, or
`push-in` alone is incomplete. A pre-03V phrase such as `room for a push-in`
describes why the still composition can support a later option; it does not
authorize the push-in.

### Screen-Job Router

This router narrows where to look; it never assigns a shot automatically.

| Current screen need | Inspect first | Reject or revise when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| identity, gaze, or micro-reaction | shot scale, clean/dirty single, lens/focus, crop | the face reads but the governing action or relation disappears |
| exchange, counteraction, or member relation | shared/single/OTS viewpoint, direction, eyeline, action axis | counterpart ownership, gaze target, or left/right relation is ambiguous |
| world, system, geography, or aftermath | wide/master relation, height, depth, layer order | scale is impressive but the member/object-to-world consequence is unreadable |
| body route, choreography, or travel | full-body scale, screen direction, lead room, edge safety | feet, destination, formation, or travel path cannot survive the frame |
| object, contact, or small state change | insert/macro relation, focus plane, match-on-action carrier | the detail adds no new causal information or cannot return to its parent event |
| reveal, concealment, or transition | depth, occluder, path, reveal target, continuity device | the still lacks the hidden surface, clearance, layer, or landing target a later move needs |
| pressure, suspension, or intentional stillness | placement, crop pressure, negative space, locked-hold feasibility | stillness is only decorative and no subject/environment change carries the cue |

### Minimum Decision Record

Use this as a thinking order, not as a new schema or parallel camera card.
Write the result into the current stage's existing natural-language fields.

```text

physical_camera_subject_world_relation:

```

Codes below are lookup handles only. Worker outputs remain natural language and
must prove the visible relation and consequence rather than list technique
names.

### Geometry Minimum

Every generation-facing camera decision names these four axes:

1. `height`: floor, knee, hip, chest, eye, high, overhead, bird/high-oblique
2. `distance_scale`: macro/insert, ECU, CU, MCU, MS, MLS, WS, EWS, two-shot,
   group
3. `direction_angle`: front, three-quarter, profile, back, OTS, low, high,
   Dutch, reflection, POV-adjacent
4. `depth`: foreground, middle, background, occlusion/reflection, negative
   space, and the subject/object layer

Also name subject placement, crop pressure, floor/reflection anchor, the
intended action axis and screen direction, motion-supporting room/layers when
relevant, and why the frame makes the cue or story state readable.

## Detailed Vocabulary Dictionary

The dimensions below are orthogonal. Shot size is not camera distance alone;
angle is not height; lens perspective is not crop; focus transfer is not camera
translation; pan is not truck; tilt is not pedestal; dolly is not zoom; arc is
not orbit. Movement and speed are also separate. At 03V, use for example
`M_PUSH + V_CREEP` or `M_PUSH + V_IMPACT`, not one speed-prefixed movement
label. Before 03V, use the movement table only to test whether the still frame
preserves the geometry a possible route would require.

### Fast Disambiguation Index

| Confused pair | Physical difference | Deciding question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| shot scale / camera distance | scale names what fits; distance also changes spatial relation and perspective | how much must read, and where must the camera physically be? |
| camera height / pitch angle | height is lens position; pitch is where it looks | is the lens low/high, looking up/down, or both? |
| crop / lens perspective | crop removes frame area; lens plus distance changes near/far size relations | is the job tighter framing or a different spatial read? |
| pan / truck | pan rotates from one position; truck translates sideways | should perspective and layer overlap change? |
| tilt / pedestal | tilt rotates vertically; pedestal translates the whole camera up/down | should the horizon/view direction or the camera height change? |
| dolly / zoom | dolly changes camera distance and perspective; zoom changes focal length from one position | should foreground/background relation change? |
| arc / orbit | arc is a partial curved move; orbit implies broader circulation around a stable anchor | how much unseen world and identity consistency must the pixels support? |
| rack focus / camera move | rack focus transfers the sharp plane without requiring camera translation | must attention move between existing depth targets, or must viewpoint change? |

### Shot scale

| Code | Family | Useful jobs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `SS_MACRO` | object/body detail | contact, texture, small state change |
| `SS_ECU` | extreme close | eye, mouth, fingertip, micro reaction |
| `SS_CU` | close | identity, gaze, emotional landing |
| `SS_MCU` | medium close | face-hand-object relation |
| `SS_MS` | medium | torso gesture, dialogue-less exchange |
| `SS_MLS` | medium long | body line, travel, formation slice |
| `SS_COWBOY` | cowboy / medium full | upper leg, hands, carried object, stance |
| `SS_FS` | full | complete body, pose, foot contact, costume silhouette |
| `SS_WS` | wide | member-to-space relation, group geometry |
| `SS_EWS` | extreme wide | world consequence, isolation, aftermath |
| `SS_EST` | establishing | location, geography, axis, arrival condition |
| `SS_2SHOT` | two-shot | exchange, counteraction, relation |
| `SS_GROUP` | group | formation, collective state, performance release |

Shot size describes how much subject/world is readable in the frame. It does
not imply emotional meaning on its own, and a later digital crop does not
recreate the perspective of moving the camera.

### Framing and viewpoint

| Code | Term | Physical meaning and useful job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `F_SINGLE` | single | one subject owns the frame; others may remain offscreen |
| `F_CLEAN_SINGLE` | clean single | no foreground shoulder/body from the counterpart |
| `F_DIRTY_SINGLE` | dirty single | counterpart edge/shoulder preserves relation and depth |
| `F_2SHOT` | two-shot | two subjects share one relational field |
| `F_3SHOT` | three-shot | three subjects share one readable hierarchy |
| `F_GROUP` | group | more than three subjects; formation and layer order must remain explicit |
| `F_OTS` | over-the-shoulder | foreground shoulder/head anchors a counterpart viewpoint and eyeline |
| `F_POV` | point of view | camera approximates what a named subject sees |
| `F_INSERT` | insert | object/body detail that carries specific story information |
| `F_CUTAWAY` | cutaway | related subject/object/environment outside the immediate action line |
| `F_MASTER` | master | geography and the whole governing action remain legible for coverage |
| `F_REFLECTION` | reflection view | reflected plane is the intentional viewing surface, not accidental duplication |

An OTS is not automatically a POV. A POV must identify whose view it is and
what visual evidence makes that ownership legible. An insert earns its place
through information or transition work, not because the sequence needs a
generic detail shot.

### Height

| Code | Height | Readable consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `H_FLOOR` | lens near floor | floor contact, stride, looming foreground, surface reflection |
| `H_KNEE` | knee height | lower-body route and ground relation |
| `H_HIP` | hip height | body/prop relation without neutral eye-level default |
| `H_CHEST` | chest height | torso/action balance and restrained human viewpoint |
| `H_EYE` | eye height | neutral relational read when earned by the scene |
| `H_HIGH` | clearly above eye line | diminished scale, pattern, vulnerability, surveillance |
| `H_OVERHEAD` | directly or near-directly above | floor plan, formation, object geometry |
| `H_BIRD` | very high aerial/bird's-eye | system/world relation over individual identity |

### Direction, pitch, and roll

| Code | Term | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `A_FRONT` | front | camera faces the subject's front plane |
| `A_34` | three-quarter | front and side planes read together |
| `A_PROFILE` | profile | lateral direction, silhouette, and eyeline become dominant |
| `A_BACK` | back / rear | destination and withholding replace face ownership |
| `A_OTS` | over-the-shoulder angle | relation and eyeline through a foreground counterpart |
| `A_LOW` | low-angle pitch | camera looks upward; height and pitch must still be named separately |
| `A_HIGH_OBLIQUE` | high oblique | camera looks down without becoming a top-down plan |
| `A_TOPDOWN` | top-down | optical axis approaches perpendicular to the ground plane |
| `A_DUTCH` | Dutch / canted roll | horizon rolls; use only for a legible instability or graphic job |
| `A_REFLECTION` | reflection | view is composed through a reflective surface |
| `A_POV_ADJ` | POV-adjacent | subjective proximity without claiming literal eyesight |

Low/high angle describes pitch toward the subject, while camera height
describes where the lens physically sits. A floor-height camera can look level;
an elevated camera can look upward. State both.

Common compound labels still require their component geometry:

- `worm's-eye`: usually `H_FLOOR` or `H_KNEE` plus a strong `A_LOW` pitch;
- `bird's-eye`: `H_BIRD` plus `A_TOPDOWN`, not merely any high angle;
- `aerial oblique`: a high aerial position with an oblique rather than
  perpendicular view;
- `neutral/on-axis`: camera sits on or close to the action axis, often to reset
  orientation before crossing it;
- `reverse angle`: the counterpart view from the other side of a relation. It
  must preserve or deliberately reset eyeline, screen direction, and the
  180-degree line.

### Temporal movement or hold — 03V vocabulary

These terms become executable only after passed pixels. The
`still-frame prerequisite` column is what 01/02A/02B may reserve without
prescribing the move.

| Code | Family | Physical change | Still-frame prerequisite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `M_HOLD_LOCK` | locked/static hold | camera remains motionless | composed action/environment motion or intentional stillness |
| `M_HOLD_DRIFT` | micro drift | tiny positional instability without a route change | crop tolerance and stable subject anchor |
| `M_PAN` | pan | camera rotates left/right from a fixed position | lateral reveal room and known start/end eyelines |
| `M_TILT` | tilt | camera rotates up/down from a fixed position | vertical reveal path and head/foot/architecture room |
| `M_PEDESTAL` | pedestal | whole camera rises/lowers without pitching | foreground/background layers and vertical clearance |
| `M_PUSH` | dolly/push in | camera physically approaches along depth | safe closing distance, subject detail, unobstructed depth path |
| `M_PULL` | dolly/pull back | camera physically retreats | world information outside the start frame and a clean retreat path |
| `M_ZOOM` | optical zoom | focal length changes while camera position stays | center/target stability and an intentional perspective-constant read |
| `M_PUNCH` | crash/punch zoom | rapid focal-length change | one clean impact target and crop margin |
| `M_RECOIL` | impact then settle | short move/zoom reverses or damps after a hit | stable landing geometry and readable impact carrier |
| `M_TRUCK_LATERAL` | truck | whole camera moves sideways | lead/travel room, lateral path, separated depth layers |
| `M_TRACK` | tracking | camera travels with or around a moving subject | visible subject route, matching speed range, edge safety |
| `M_FOLLOW` | follow | camera trails, leads, or parallels a subject | clear travel vector, destination room, stable subject scale intent |
| `M_ARC` | arc | camera travels on a partial curved path around a relation | layered background and enough side/rear visual truth |
| `M_ORBIT` | orbit | camera circles a stable anchor more fully | central anchor, 360-degree-consistent world/identity, occlusion plan |
| `M_CRANE_UP` | crane/boom rise | camera translates upward through space | vertical clearance and widening depth layers |
| `M_CRANE_DOWN` | crane/boom descend | camera translates downward through space | readable landing target and controlled occlusion |
| `M_AXIS_DRIVE` | depth-axis move | camera travels forward/back along the action axis | corridor/threshold geometry and vanishing-point stability |
| `M_FIELD_SWEEP` | wide sweep | broad pan/truck/crane reveals a larger system | ordered reveal targets rather than unspecified scenery |
| `M_WHIP_PAN` | whip pan | very fast pan produces directional blur | strong exit/entry vector and a motivated hidden transition |
| `M_WHIP_TILT` | whip tilt | very fast tilt produces vertical blur | strong vertical target and safe start/end crops |
| `M_ROLL` | camera roll | camera rotates around the lens axis | centered readable anchor and a deliberate horizon consequence |
| `M_DOLLY_ZOOM` | dolly zoom | camera and focal length change oppositely | stable subject scale, deep background, physically plausible path |
| `M_PARALLAX` | parallax result | relative layer speed reveals depth during translation | distinct foreground/middle/background planes |
| `M_RACK_FOCUS` | focus transfer | focus shifts between depth planes; camera need not move | at least two separated, meaningful focus targets |

`pan`/`tilt` rotate the camera; `truck`/`pedestal` translate it. `dolly`
changes camera-to-subject distance and perspective; `zoom` changes focal length
from one position. `arc` is a partial curved route; `orbit` implies a more
complete circulation around a stable anchor. `rack focus` changes attention,
not camera position.

### Rig and motion character

Rig words describe motion texture or physical access, not the route by
themselves:

- `R_TRIPOD_LOCK`: stable locked support;
- `R_DOLLY`: smooth constrained translation;
- `R_JIB_CRANE`: vertical or compound elevated translation;
- `R_HANDHELD`: operator-borne micro-instability and reactive correction;
- `R_SHOULDER`: heavier handheld body rhythm;
- `R_STEADICAM`: operator movement with stabilized floating inertia;
- `R_GIMBAL`: electronically stabilized, often cleaner directional following;
- `R_DRONE_AERIAL`: free elevated travel whose altitude, heading, and target
  still need specification;
- `R_FPV`: highly directional flight through/around obstacles; requires a
  physically legible path and is high risk for image-to-video consistency;
- `R_BODY_MOUNT`: camera is rigidly attached to the subject (Snorricam/body
  mount), keeping the face/body unusually stable while the world moves around
  it; identity edges and the full surrounding route must be supportable.

Do not write only `handheld`, `drone`, or `gimbal shot`. Pair the rig character
with route, direction, speed, subject relation, stop, and screen job in 03V.

Transition devices such as object occlusion, reflection match, axis match,
value wipe, carried-vector match, long-lens compression, and deliberate hard
reset are recorded as handoff or lens/composition decisions rather than
disguised as ordinary camera movement.

### Speed and envelope

Use one speed for every movement and for intentional holds:

`V_STILL`, `V_CREEP`, `V_SLOW`, `V_MED`, `V_BRISK`, `V_FAST`,
`V_IMPACT`.

Optional envelopes describe how speed changes inside the shot:

`E_CONSTANT`, `E_EASE_IN`, `E_EASE_OUT`, `E_HIT_STOP`, `E_BREATH`,
`E_STUTTER`, `E_RAMP_UP`, `E_RAMP_DOWN`, `E_PULSE`.

The selected audio decides the pairing. A restrained section can use a fast
one-shot impact; a high-energy section can use a long locked hold when the stop
is the musical event. Do not map section names to fixed speed families.

### Composition

`C_CENTER_LOCK`, `C_RULE_THIRDS`, `C_TRIANGLE`, `C_FG_DOMINANT`,
`C_NEGATIVE_SPACE`, `C_FRAME_IN_FRAME`, `C_LEADING_LINES`, `C_SYMMETRY`,
`C_DIAGONAL`, `C_TOPDOWN_GEOMETRY`, `C_SPLIT_FIELD`, `C_STACK_DEPTH`,
`C_TINY_IN_FIELD`, `C_CROP_PRESSURE`.

Also author the practical margins that control both still generation and later
motion:

- `headroom`: space above the head or deliberate top-crop pressure;
- `look_room`: space in front of gaze;
- `lead_room` / `travel_room`: space in front of a moving subject or camera
  reveal direction;
- `edge_safety`: body, prop, hair, wardrobe, or architecture that must not be
  cut by expected reframing;
- `frame_within_frame`: doorway, window, reflection, or object enclosure whose
  foreground ownership is explicit;
- `layer_order`: foreground, subject/action plane, middle consequence, and
  background destination;
- `occlusion_plan`: what may cover the lens/subject, when, and what it reveals;
- `vanishing_point`: the depth direction the camera/subject route must respect.

Rule of thirds, symmetry, leading lines, and negative space are starting
relationships, not quality words. Name the subject placement, line/shape that
does the work, and continuity consequence.

### Lens, perspective, depth of field, and focus

`L_MACRO_SHALLOW`, `L_PORTRAIT`, `L_NORMAL`, `L_WIDE_NEAR`,
`L_LONG_COMPRESS`, `L_WIDE_FIELD`.

Lens feel describes the spatial read. It is not a brand or focal-length fetish.
Use these consequences in natural language:

- `wide-near`: expanded near/far distance, stronger foreground size, deeper
  travel feeling, and higher edge/distortion risk;
- `normal`: restrained perspective without conspicuous expansion/compression;
- `long-compressed`: flattened apparent spacing, isolated planes, and reduced
  lateral/depth travel tolerance;
- `macro`: very close detail with narrow focus tolerance;
- `deep focus`: foreground, subject, and background stay readable together;
- `shallow focus`: one plane dominates while other planes soften;
- `soft focus`: deliberately reduced edge/acutance character, not missed
  focus;
- `rack focus`: a 03V attention transfer between two named depth targets.

Stylized but common lens/plane techniques remain optional and physically
defined:

- `L_ULTRAWIDE_FISHEYE`: extreme field of view with deliberate barrel
  distortion and edge stretching; protect faces/limbs from accidental edge
  deformation;
- `L_ANAMORPHIC_WIDE`: wide horizontal field, oval/elliptical defocus and
  horizontal flare only when those visible traits are actually wanted;
- `L_SPLIT_DIOPTER`: near and far subjects remain sharp while an intentional
  transition/seam divides the focus planes;
- `L_TILT_SHIFT`: a selectively tilted focus plane or miniature-like scale
  read; name which plane stays sharp and why.

Focal length, camera-to-subject distance, aperture/depth of field, and sensor or
crop are different controls. If a numeric focal length is useful, pair it with
the intended perspective and field-of-view consequence; never assume a number
alone will produce the desired shot.

## Motion Readiness Before 03V

01/02A/02B must make a good still first, but the still must not trap the later
video. For every Branch Cut expected to carry meaningful temporal change,
author the applicable readiness conditions inside the existing natural-language
camera, composition, depth, continuity, and edit-use fields:

1. `motion_carrier_hypothesis`: subject, camera, environment/object/material,
   intentional hold, or a reasoned combination. This is a feasibility test,
   never a locked temporal move.
2. `action_axis_and_screen_direction`: who/what travels or looks which way and
   which camera side preserves the read.
3. `lead_travel_reveal_room`: the visible empty or occupied area needed in the
   likely direction of action, pan/tilt reveal, or reframing.
4. `depth_and_parallax_layers`: at least the layers that could separate during
   translation, or an explicit flat/graphic intention.
5. `physical_path_and_occlusion`: clear route, threshold, foreground wipe,
   reflection, obstacle, or the reason no route is needed.
6. `start_state_and_edge_safety`: crop, limb/prop/wardrobe boundaries, gaze,
   and composition anchors that must survive the first motion phase.
7. `reveal_or_stop_target`: the subject, world fact, contact, alignment, or
   empty field that would justify a later landing.
8. `continuity_carrier`: vector, eyeline, action phase, object, shape, color,
   light, occlusion, scale, or deliberate discontinuity into the neighbor.
9. `supportable_and_unsupported_families`: only when useful, state which broad
   motion families the geometry could or could not support and why. Do not
   specify speed, envelope, exact path, or stop as an upstream instruction.

If the image needs a face on the extreme edge while the likely subject route
continues out of frame, or if an orbit would expose an un-authored rear world,
the frame is not motion-ready for that option. Change the still composition or
defer/choose another option; never expect 03V wording to invent missing space.

## Continuity And Edit Vocabulary

Continuity is not generic visual similarity. Name the exact carrier and whether
it is preserved, transformed, or intentionally broken.

| Term | Meaning | What upstream stills must prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `action_axis / 180-degree rule` | keep cameras on one side of the governing action line so left/right relations remain stable | action line, camera side, subject screen direction; an axis crossing needs a visible reset or deliberate break |
| `30-degree rule` | a cut between similar views needs a substantial angle or scale change to avoid a jump-like duplicate | why the new Branch Cut adds information, not only a tiny viewpoint change |
| `screen-direction match` | travel/gaze/object vector continues coherently across the boundary | exit vector and next entry vector |
| `eyeline match` | a look is followed by a view whose position/direction answers it | exact gaze direction, target height/side, and target ownership |
| `match on action` | the cut bridges one action across phases | readable action phase before and after, with body/object contact continuity |
| `eye trace` | the next important subject appears near the viewer's current attention point | attention anchor and next-frame target placement |
| `graphic match` | shape, silhouette, line, scale, or framing carries the join | named matching geometry and intended difference |
| `color/light/material match` | value, hue, light direction, highlight, or material reaction bridges the join | named visible surface, not a vague palette similarity |
| `object/contact match` | one object, touch point, or consequence carries causality | object state and contact phase on both sides |
| `occlusion wipe` | foreground/object/body covers the image and reveals the next state | occluder path, coverage area, reveal target, and whether the wipe is camera- or subject-led |
| `vector/velocity carry` | direction and apparent speed continue through the edit | exit direction, entry direction, and relative motion character |
| `scale or depth release` | compressed/close space opens or the reverse | start/end scale, layer relation, and why the change matters |
| `intentional axis reset` | geography is re-established before/while changing sides | reset view, neutral-on-axis bridge, or explicit disorientation job |
| `deliberate discontinuity` | jump, orientation break, or hard graphic collision is the intended meaning | exact rule being broken, audience effect, and recovery/next-state logic |

The 180- and 30-degree rules are continuity tools, not automatic bans. A
purposeful break is valid when the section states the readable consequence and
how the viewer reorients—or why disorientation is the point.

## Common Coverage Grammar

These are professional coverage relations, not fixed Branch-Cut counts:

| Term | Use | Planning requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `master-and-coverage` | establish the full action/geography, then use closer or alternate views for information and emphasis | master must prove geography; closer views must add a distinct viewer read |
| `shot/reverse-shot` | alternate relational viewpoints, often singles or OTS views | matched eyelines, screen sides, action phase, and a reason for each reverse |
| `cross coverage` | cameras cover opposing participants simultaneously or as matched counterparts | consistent axis, focal/scale logic, and counterpart visibility |
| `insert / cut-in` | move closer to an object/body detail inside the same action | exact information gained and continuity back to the parent action |
| `cutaway / reaction` | leave the main action for a related witness, consequence, environment, or object | causal or rhythmic job plus a clean return or onward handoff |
| `reveal shot` | camera/subject/occluder gradually exposes new information | start concealment, reveal mechanism, target, timing owner, and stop/readability condition |
| `oner / sequence shot` | one sustained temporal take carries several beats without an edit | complete physical path, blocking, focus/depth changes, transitions inside the take, and a feasible endpoint |

Do not turn `shot/reverse-shot` into automatic dialogue grammar or use a master
as a compulsory opening. The current song, screen event, reference function,
and edit consequence decide whether the coverage relation is useful.

## Coverage Patterns

Patterns are reusable relations, not a mandatory sequence:

- `proof_sandwich`: detail -> owner/reaction -> changed detail
- `relation_ladder`: viewpoint A -> shared object/action -> viewpoint B
- `layer_reveal`: locked subject/object -> attention moves to another depth
  layer -> return or consequence
- `scale_release`: compressed frame -> wider consequence -> human/object reset
- `claim_descent`: wider observation -> action detail -> low/floor landing ->
  consequence
- `pressure_hold`: compressed or negative-space hold -> micro change -> hard
  release
- `axis_expansion`: depth-axis move -> world/system reveal -> clean stop
- `exit_reverse`: final state -> release detail -> reverse route or empty
  afterimage

Name the pattern's current-song job and modify it freely. Never repeat a pattern
because it appeared in a prior run.

## Contextual Anti-Template Review

No camera tuple, repeated word, semantic-similarity score, reference frequency,
or fixed consecutive window may pass or fail creative direction. Those signals
cannot distinguish a deliberate hold, match cut, boundary carry, response,
reprise, escalation, or flat reuse. Every 02A section is therefore reviewed by
the active LLM owner with its Core-Cut/Branch-Cut event ownership and adjacent
boundaries, then
the merged video receives one whole-video reconciliation.

The LLM review should challenge, without mechanically rejecting:

- every section resolving to front eye-level CU plus push
- camera motion used to imitate song energy without a screen job
- constant orbit, Dutch angle, handheld shake, or white-flash transition as a
  default style
- a new angle that changes nothing the viewer can read
- inherited project objects, locations, colors, section ids, or route names in
  this generic skill
- dense Branch-Cut splitting used to fake camera coverage inside one continuous
  event
- a still that looks polished alone but leaves no crop margin, depth layer,
  visible path, or reveal target for its intended video job
- a cut pair whose eyeline, action axis, or exit/entry vector reverses by
  accident
- a 03V prompt that stacks several incompatible camera routes instead of one
  coherent cue

## Active Stage And Field Mapping

Use the current schema fields first. Do not create a parallel camera card.

### P3 / internal FP Close

Author four-axis still geometry on Core-bound change-point slots in the kinetic
slot sheet (`kinetic_change_point_slot_sheet.v1`). Sequence observations stay
evidence. Key Screen inherits the Core-bound slot camera and action; do not
re-author composition in Key Screen and do not convert Key Screen, Core slots,
or `scene_phases` into the slot sheet.

### 01

Author `still_camera_coverage` for every generation-facing section using the
active 01 schema:

- `screen_readability_job` states what the viewer must understand;
- `shot_scale_distance`, `camera_height`, `direction_angle`, and
  `depth_design` state the still geometry;
- `subject_placement` and `crop_negative_space` state the frame;
- `variation_reason` states why this view differs or intentionally repeats;
- `temporal_camera_owner` remains
  `03V_after_passed_image_pixel_inspection`;
- `continuity_in`, `continuity_out`, branching opportunities, and transition
  fields carry motion-readiness and edit consequences in natural language.

Do not author a temporal `movement_family`, `base_speed`, `envelope`, or stop
inside new 01 creative truth. If a compatibility working slice contains
`camera_direction.v2`, its temporal-looking keys are non-authorizing historical
transport only; they must not enter canonical 01 or a 02A Branch Cut.

### 02A

Use the parent Core Cut's `camera_coverage_plan.spatial_anchor_and_axis`,
`anchor_view`, `branch_camera_roles`, `protected_continuity`, and
`allowed_variation`. Each Branch Cut's exact still-camera, `camera_treatment`,
`continuity_in`, `continuity_out`, and edit use remain authoritative. Put the
applicable motion-readiness facts in those natural-language fields without
inventing an unregistered key or temporal instruction.

### 02B

The 02B owner does not load this full vocabulary or select another camera idea.
Its compact translation detail carries
`Visible details.still_camera_and_composition`, which exact-translates the
passed still geometry and only visible motion prerequisites: lead/travel/reveal
room, foreground/middle/background separation, safe crop, clear
path/threshold, occluder, and start-state anchor when applicable. It does not
write pan, push, orbit, speed, easing, or an endpoint. `Action cue` stays a
visible still state.

### 03V

After pixel inspection, compare the passed image, audio placement, selected
reference motion grammar, and upstream continuity/readiness conditions. Then
author one coherent temporal route: movement or locked hold, direction, rig
character when useful, speed, envelope, depth/parallax change, reveal/stop,
readability condition, and edit handoff.

Before that temporal route, every distinct generated production image on
the planning/production storyboard (Official Core primaries and Branch
stills, not only a 03V 1--3 packet) must already have a model-authored
full-collection camera-angle review. Facing-and-pitch (`direction_angle` /
Core `camera_angle`) is the angle; shot size, height-only, labels,
adjacent-only comparison, random rotation, numerical similarity, and
machine family-matching do not satisfy that duty. Same image exact-carry
is not a new image. This still-image duty is separate from later temporal
movement-family variation. A readiness hypothesis is not a lock;
actual pixels may prove it unsupported, in which case choose another evidence-
supported route or return to the smallest upstream owner.

The active 03V schema keeps a broad routing enum while natural-language fields
carry the precise physical technique. Do not place an unregistered `M_*` code
in `camera_motion_route.movement_family`. Map it truthfully and retain the
specific term in `movement_direction`/prompt prose:

| Physical vocabulary | Active `movement_family` route |
| --- | --- |
| locked/static or micro-drift hold | `locked_hold` |
| dolly/push in | `push` |
| dolly/pull back | `pull` |
| tracking or lateral truck | `track` |
| pan or whip pan | `pan` |
| tilt or whip tilt | `tilt` |
| arc or orbit | `orbit` |
| subject lead/trail/parallel follow | `follow` |
| handheld/shoulder motion character | `handheld` |
| crane, boom, or pedestal translation | `crane` |
| rack focus without camera translation | `rack_focus` |
| zoom, crash zoom, recoil, roll, dolly zoom, FPV, or a genuinely compound route | `combined_motivated_route` |

The enum routes validation; it never replaces direction, rig character, speed,
depth behavior, reveal/stop, readability, or edit-handoff prose.

## Prompt Assembly Grammar

These are placeholder grammars, not copy-ready scene prose.

For 02B image translation:

```text
[shot scale/distance] + [camera height] + [direction/pitch/roll] +
[subject placement and crop] + [foreground/middle/background or deliberate
flatness] + [lens/focus consequence] + [lead/travel/reveal room and edge safety
when applicable] + [continuity anchor] + [why the still reads the screen job]
```

For 03V image-to-video direction:

```text
[inherited start view] + [audio/start trigger] + [subject/object/environment
motion or intentional hold] + [one camera movement/hold family and direction] +
[speed and envelope] + [depth/parallax or focus behavior] + [revealed target or
stop condition] + [readability protection] + [continuity/edit handoff]
```

Author the essential motion first, then add only the subject, camera, or scene
motion needed to remove ambiguity. Prefer positive physical wording such as
`the camera remains locked` to a negative-only phrase such as `no camera
movement`. For a dramatic change, state what is visible before, during, and
after the reveal rather than stacking technique names. Image-to-video text
focuses on motion because the passed image already controls appearance,
composition, color, light, and style.

## 02A To 03V Transfer Rules

- 02A allocates coverage; it does not reopen 01 direction or author temporal
  camera movement.
- 02A may compare concrete still views and motion-supporting conditions, but
  every selected Branch Cut keeps geometry, composition, lens/focus feel,
  readiness, continuity, and readability job distinct.
- 02B translates that selected still view and its visible readiness conditions;
  it may not invent a new angle, movement, speed, endpoint, or framing relation.
- 03V does not blindly execute an upstream move name. It inspects actual pixels
  and chooses the strongest supported temporal route while preserving the
  protected action axis, start view, screen direction, visibility, and edit
  handoff.
- If a desired 03V route requires unseen world surfaces, impossible camera
  travel, missing depth layers, unsafe crops, or a changed passed state, HOLD
  and return to the smallest owning still-image stage.

## Research Basis And Scope

This working catalog covers the common high-utility families used in film,
commercial, music-video, and current image-to-video prompting. It is broad,
not closed: a director may use a more specific professional term when its
physical camera/subject relation and screen job are also stated.

The terminology and prompting guidance were reconciled on 2026-08-09 against:

- Runway's official camera-term and Gen-4 prompting guides for shot size,
  angle, movement, focus, positive camera-lock wording, and motion-first I2V
  prompting;
- Google Cloud's official Veo prompting guide for the
  cinematography/subject/action/context/style decomposition and first/last
  frame motion control;
- Adobe's cinematography and continuity-editing guidance for shot/angle
  meaning, sequence coverage, the 180-degree rule, eyeline match, match on
  action, and eye trace; and
- ZEISS optics guidance for focal length, subject distance, perspective, and
  depth-of-field consequences.
