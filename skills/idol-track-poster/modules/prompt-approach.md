# Prompt Approach for Track Posters

## Golden Rule

The selected Front Planning plan/canvas owns the world, wardrobe/material, and
role intent. **Approved actual reference images own identity during this
asset-production job, while their bound interpretation receipts own selected
visual consequences.** The resulting passed current-title visual, not those
production inputs, is the downstream MV appearance source.

The assigned current-title identity/track-poster author under CDD writes a new
or meaning-changing production prompt after reading the active phase source,
verifying current-title identity evidence, and consuming the selected visual/
space/object/camera receipts. One explicitly authorized bounded forensic adviser
may inspect one named actual-pixel or returned-image risk and return
observable facts, locked-rubric compliance, technical defects, uncertainty,
and a reversible technical pass/hold/rerun to the assigned author and CDD. The
adviser never writes prompt prose, selects among creatively valid candidates,
or fills or reinterprets missing creative truth.
It inspects a reference again only when its
`inspection_action` requires a new/revised receipt. A prompt may create only a
noncanonical candidate; Founder selection, Front Planning integration, CDD
review of the exact integrated bytes, and Overall promotion are separate
requirements.
Scripts and fixed skeletons do not choose pose, camera, space, wardrobe, or
reference application. Provider attachment follows the separate security and
approval boundary; do not claim a locally inspected image is attached.

## Prompt Structure (Recommended Order)

1. One accurate sentence for the actually attached identity/reference inputs
2. Track and member/space/object role from the current plan
3. Specific space and selected actual-reference consequence
4. Exact wardrobe/material lane when a member is visible
5. One clear pose, action, or intentional hold from the current track
6. Camera geometry, placement, crop, and poster negative space
7. Lighting that unifies person, object, material, and environment
8. Reference discard locks plus observed identity/render failure risks

## Tips That Work Well

- Name the specific space and 1-2 concrete objects from it.
- Describe the garment details as "timing tools" or "reacting to the rhythm".
- Spend real sentences on how the light hits both the person and the environment together.
- For pose: describe weight distribution + one key hand/foot/garment reaction + gaze + torso angle. This is more effective than vague "cool pose".

## What Usually Breaks Track Posters

- Face gets pretty beauty lighting while the world is cold → pasted look
- Wardrobe becomes generic black tactical outfit instead of the specific lane
- Pose is just standing with good fashion posture (no rhythm information)
- Background is too vague or too busy
- Body proportions drift because the prompt was trying too hard to make a "sexy/cool full body shot"
