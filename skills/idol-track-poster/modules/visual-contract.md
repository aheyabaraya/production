# Track Poster Visual Contract

## What a Track Poster Is

A single, powerful, iconic full-body (or strong 3/4 full body) image that functions as the official visual signature of one specific track.

It is the "hero still" that represents the track's creative nucleus — world + wardrobe system + performance language + member identity — in one readable image.

## Required Qualities

1. **Instant Member Recognition**
   The person must be unmistakably the same as in their character board and clean portrait.

2. **Instant Track Recognition**
   A candidate must read clearly against the selected Front Planning
   plan/canvas. A canonical asset must read clearly against the passed
   current-title visual package through space, wardrobe silhouette, and body
   language.

3. **Poster Strength**
   Works at small size (thumbnail, card, social post) and large size (poster, key art, screen).
   Strong silhouette, clear gesture, good negative space, intentional composition.

4. **Controlled Power**
   The pose carries real weight and rhythm implication, but it is a static, composed asset — not a captured action frame from the MV.

5. **Interpretation Continuity**
   The image realizes the selected receipt's world, camera, object, material,
   and section function. A later model may not reinterpret that reference merely
   because poster authoring happens in another session.

## LOW-Specific Contract (Historical Example Only)

- **World DNA**: Cold after-hours airport access control (fluorescent + overcast window + metal reflections + floor lines + restricted objects like scanner frames, cargo belts, shutters) as source provenance.
- **Wardrobe DNA**: Elastic Body Signal system — fitted dark base + cropped utility layer + cargo/skort elements + compact hardware + narrow motion details (tie, scarf tail, strap, sleeve edge, cropped hem). Clothes are timing tools.
- **Body Language DNA**: Low pressure, compact, grounded, elastic rebound, garment stop on beat, low gaze, precise weight shifts, boot contact.

A good LOW track poster shows a member inside one of the approved airport spaces, correctly dressed in their assigned lane, in a strong full-body stance or light action where the clothing and body answer the low, controlled, rhythmic language of the track.

This is a record of LOW's historical locks. It is organization/depth evidence
only. For any other track, replace the world, wardrobe, body language, lighting,
and framing with that track's passed Front Planning package.

## Lighting Rule (Non-Negotiable)

The face, neck, torso, limbs, wardrobe, and the immediate surrounding environment must share the same light direction, color temperature, and quality.

Separate beauty lighting on the face while the body and world have cold airport light = immediate failure. The figure must belong to the space.

## Framing Recommendations

- Full body (head to toe) when the boot/foot contact and floor line are important to the track language.
- Strong three-quarter full body when torso, hand gesture, and garment reaction are the hero elements.
- Always leave enough negative space for text overlay if needed later (title, member name, etc.).

## What Track Posters Are NOT

- Clean beauty portraits (wrong scale, wrong wardrobe, wrong world)
- MV production stills (too much action, too cinematic, too much story information)
- Fashion lookbook shots (wardrobe is the star, not the integrated track system)
- Character renders (no real lighting integration with a specific world)
