# Director-Room Sequence Discovery

Status: active IDOL planning method; retained CF examples are historical only
Scope: IDOL Front Planning. Teaser follows its existing active IDOL route.
CF production is inactive; CF-specific passages below are for explicit archive
inspection and never authorize a new CF planning or approval lane.
Authority: `AGENTS.md`, the active lane `AGENTS.md`, owning policy, schema, and
owning skill remain higher authority

## Purpose

This method turns a loose creative conversation into a coherent screen
sequence before Graph Engineering formalizes it. It preserves the successful working
pattern in which the Founder can speak in ordinary directing language and the
assigned director converts those notes into observable screen structure.

It is not a new production stage, fixed questionnaire, scene-count rule, or
substitute for source inspection. IDOL saves startup authorization and the
director's recommended flow once in
`schemas/idol-founder-sequence-control.schema.json` (v3), projecting into
existing owning fields without asking the Founder to fill a schema. The retained
CF `schemas/founder-sequence-control.schema.json` (v1) is retired evidence;
`scripts/validate_founder_sequence_control.rb --mode historical_audit` reads it
only for an explicitly requested archive inspection. Current authoring rejects it.

## When To Activate

Use the loop when the current direction contains one or more of these:

- an adaptation premise without a complete current-song or current-subject
  screen flow;
- references plus disconnected actions, objects, spaces, members, or styling;
- a partial arrow sequence whose pacing, ownership, or transitions are not
  resolved;
- an attractive scene order with no observable message or commercial reason;
- a sequence whose source grammar has not yet been bound to exact ranges and
  screen functions.

If the Founder already supplied a complete, internally consistent sequence,
the director audits it against this method, reports only material gaps, and
projects it without manufacturing extra iterations.

## Director Behavior

The director leads. Do not ask the Founder to fill the canonical schema or
answer a long intake form when the available evidence supports a professional
first proposal. Codex or Grok transcribes the ordinary-language conversation
into the lane's `Founder Sequence Control` and shows the compact whole flow and
reference-use map. For IDOL, the control becomes director-ready from explicit
startup authorization and is not a separate approval gate; the director carries
the complete recommendation into the consolidated Front Planning review. Archived CF
evidence retains its recorded passed-control gate; that history supplies no
current CF authoring authority.

1. Preserve the Founder wording and distinguish preferences from explicit
   locks.
2. Inventory the usable screen ingredients: audio turns or commercial beats,
   subjects and members, actions and holds, objects, spaces, relations, direct
   reference sequences, camera/composition opportunities, styling, sound, and
   continuity constraints.
3. Propose the first rough sequence in timeline order. Every beat needs a
   dominant job; a list of attractive shots is not yet a sequence.
4. Challenge the proposal before calling it director-ready.
5. Translate any ordinary Founder corrections into structural revisions and
   show the complete affected sequence again; do not require this extra loop
   when the Founder has asked for the director's first full proposal.
6. Ask one focused question only when a real unresolved choice would materially
   change the route and cannot be decided from current evidence.

## Rough Beat Truth

At draft depth, each beat states enough to judge the flow without pretending to
be a final storyboard:

- dominant story, character, relation, world, performance, commercial, proof,
  transition, or afterimage job;
- entry state;
- visible action, reaction, or intentional hold;
- what changes on screen and the beat endpoint;
- subject, member, object, and space ownership;
- governing reference range or function when used;
- camera/read contribution at the level needed to distinguish the beat;
- sound opportunity or intentional silence;
- continuity into and out of neighboring beats.

The number of beats follows the material. Do not copy a previous run's count,
timings, member allocation, props, maps, weapons, products, or camera pattern.

## Mandatory Director Challenge

Before presenting a draft as ready, test:

- **Pacing:** Is one setup or action carrying too much time or too many jobs?
- **Ownership:** Does one member, product, object, or world rule perform nearly
  every decisive action while others become decoration?
- **Repetition:** Are neighboring beats repeating the same carrier, scale,
  action, proof, caption, or thesis?
- **Reference integrity:** Has a selected sequence been reduced to a
  representative still or had its ordered grammar rearranged without reason?
- **Message:** Does meaning emerge from visible causality, relation, contrast,
  choice, consequence, sound, or space, or has a slogan merely been pasted on?
- **Commercial necessity (historical CF audit only):** In a retained CF record, would the scene still work if the advertised
  subject were removed or replaced by a generic prop?
- **Misread and feasibility:** Can the action, identity, claim, safety, or world
  state be read incorrectly or become impractical downstream?
- **Continuity:** Does each endpoint create a usable reason for the next beat,
  including changes of space, direction, object state, member state, and sound?

The director may remove, compress, split, reorder, or reassign a beat when the
challenge exposes a concrete screen problem. Explain the consequence in plain
language.

## Message Discovery

Do not require a slogan before exploring the sequence. First inspect what the
screen relationships already say: who initiates, who answers, what is shared,
what separates, what changes hands, what survives impact, what the world rewards
or rejects, and how sound connects or interrupts those events.

Then state the message in one sentence and verify that several different beats
carry different parts of it. A message may be seeded, embodied, complicated,
withheld, released, resolved, or left as an afterimage. It must not become the
same explanatory beat repeated throughout the video.

For historical CF audit, the retained discovery remains subordinate to advertised-subject need, proof,
objection, desired action, and claim safety. A poetic message cannot replace a
commercial argument.

## Founder Review Loop

Present the Founder with:

1. one-line premise and proposed message;
2. the complete ordered rough sequence;
3. the director's named problems and the revisions already made;
4. only the remaining decisions that materially change the route.

When the Founder says that an action is too long, a member owns too much, a
transition is weak, a reference should govern a different beat, or the message
is missing, record the wording at the smallest owning surface. Rebuild the
affected sequence and show its whole local flow, not only an isolated patch.

For IDOL, this material appears inside the one consolidated Front Planning
PASS/HOLD/REJECT. The only earlier mandatory Founder action is startup
authorization, plus an exception choice for a new/restyled member visual or an
unresolved material conflict. Archived CF passed controls remain evidence only;
new CF sequence-control approval is inactive. Neither history nor IDOL startup authorizes image generation, paid work, public
release, or bypasses later storyboard, pixel, playback, or edit gates.

## PASS Conditions

The discovery is ready to project when:

- the complete sequence can be read in order without relying on missing prose;
- every beat has a distinct dominant job, visible change or intentional hold,
  endpoint, and continuity reason;
- pacing, ownership, repetition, reference order, message, sound, misread risk,
  and feasibility are resolved or explicitly held;
- selected direct sequences retain their ordered grammar and exact intended
  function;
- every IDOL Major and reference proposal has been transcribed in the director-
  ready control, explicit Founder locks are preserved, and no material startup
  choice remains unresolved; retained CF decisions are inspected against their
  historical Founder review, without authoring or approving new CF work;
- IDOL meaning progresses across the video; an explicit historical CF audit
  checks the commercial causality in the retained evidence; and
- the Founder can judge the direction without filling downstream forms.
